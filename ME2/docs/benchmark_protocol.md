# Benchmark protocol

## Frozen data

The pinned Hugging Face revision contains 4,443 test records and 202 holdout
rows. Test membership is retained exactly. The prepared holdout contains 201
records because two source rows contain the same Common Voice recording; the
later duplicate is removed through a checksummed exclusion sidecar so it
cannot receive double weight. Reviewed label corrections and the exclusion are
applied without editing the source release. The holdout is never available to training, HPO,
calibration, architecture selection, rejection selection, or test comparison.

The original training split plus approved real samples from the previous
release form the development pool. Supplemental recordings are admitted only
after excluding evaluation speakers, duplicate source identities, and exact
decoded-audio duplicates. Reviewed relabeling is an in-place transformation:
an accepted recording is removed from the unsupported pool and appears once
under its reviewed canonical label.

## Development split

A deterministic grouped search selects approximately 15% of reliable speakers
for validation. Every reliable speaker remains entirely in train or validation,
and repeated takes from that speaker cannot cross the boundary. Validation is
real speech only. Synthetic recordings and data without trustworthy speaker
identity remain training-only.

All validation labels require at least five recordings except `MESSAGE`.
The updated release contains only seven real, speaker-identified `MESSAGE`
development recordings across four speakers, so its predeclared feasible floor
is two recordings, two speakers, and two recording groups in each development
partition. The selected validation split contains three recordings from two
speakers; this limitation must accompany per-intent validation results. Because reminder data
was initially concentrated in two speakers, `CREATE_REMINDER` also requires at
least five speakers and ten independent recording groups in both train and
validation. The split search fails rather than silently weakening these gates.

This is primarily a speaker-generalization experiment. Identical command text
spoken by different people may cross splits; that is expected for this research
question and is not acoustic leakage.

## Data ablations

All models use the same validation and test records. Five declared training
conditions isolate the effects of benchmark synthetic audio, external real
supplementation, and the separate supplemental-TTS shard:

| Condition | Training records |
|---|---|
| `benchmark_real` | frozen source, real only |
| `benchmark_mixed` | frozen source, real and synthetic |
| `supplemented_real` | frozen real plus screened previous real |
| `supplemented_mixed` | benchmark mixed data plus screened previous real audio |
| `supplemented_mixed_extended` | `supplemented_mixed` plus the supplemental TTS shard's `voice_split=train` records |

No on-the-fly TTS is generated by this pipeline. Supplemental TTS records
assigned to the source shard's test and holdout voice partitions are never
materialized, so they cannot enter training or either frozen evaluation set.

## Rejection and thresholds

Unsupported commands are an execution-rejection outcome, not a twentieth
canonical action. Three strategies are compared: calibrated confidence,
an experimental internal UNKNOWN output, and a binary in-scope head.

Temperature and the in-scope threshold are fitted on validation only. The
threshold maximizes in-scope recall subject to a pre-registered unsupported
false-accept-rate ceiling of 5%. This replaces an arbitrary fixed threshold.
The chosen threshold is frozen for test and holdout.

## Reporting and selection

Report intent accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro/micro/weighted precision,
recall and F1, per-intent results, confusion matrices, calibration error, NLL,
Brier score, slot strict/normalized precision-recall-F1, character error rate,
joint intent-slot accuracy, unsupported FAR, in-scope FRR, AUROC, AUPRC, model
size, parameter count, ONNX parity, and CPU latency.

HPO and early stopping use validation only. Candidate selection first enforces
the registered rejection constraints, then ranks intent macro F1, joint
intent-slot accuracy, model size, and latency. Finalists are replicated with
three seeds. The locked test chooses the final model; bootstrap intervals
measure test-sample uncertainty, while variation across seeds measures
optimization uncertainty. The sealed holdout is then assessed once on the
Raspberry Pi 5, including real-time factor and latency.
